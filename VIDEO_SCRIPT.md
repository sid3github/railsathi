# Video script (read this out loud)

Written to sound like you're talking to a friend, not reading a report. Read it
once or twice before recording so it feels like your own words, not a script.

**[Bracketed lines]** are actions (what to click). Everything else is what you say.

Total spoken words: ~264. At a relaxed, conversational pace that's about 1:45–1:50 —
under the 2:00 limit, so you don't need to rush.

---

### MINUTE 1 — show the app

**[Open the homepage.]**
"Hey, I'm Sid. This is RailSathi."

"Here's the problem: your train gets cancelled, and all you get is a text message. Then you're stuck figuring out what to do next."

**[Type the PNR.]**
"Let me show you. I just type in my PNR — no login, no password."

**[Click "View my plan."]**
"One tap, and it tells me what's going on."

**[Cancellation screen shows.]**
"My train's cancelled. RailSathi explains it in plain language, and tells me right away — my money's safe."

**[Scroll to the three option cards.]**
"Then it gives me three real choices — take another train, get a refund, or talk to someone."

**[Click "See train options." Pick a different train.]**
"I choose another train. I can see the time, the seat, and any extra cost — before I commit to anything."

**[Click "Confirm my new plan."]**
"One more tap, and I'm done. New ticket confirmed, refund already on its way."

**[Tap the language button (हि). Let it switch on screen.]**
"And the whole app also works in Hindi — not just a few words, everything."

---

### MINUTE 2 — how it was built

**[Show the code editor or the Codex session.]**
"Now, how I built it. I used Codex for pretty much everything — the code, the screens, all of it."

**[Show the network panel, or just say the numbers on screen.]**
"Speed mattered a lot to me. This page started at over a megabyte. Now it's under 300 kilobytes — because a lot of people here are on slower connections."

**[Tab through a screen with the keyboard, so the focus outline shows.]**
"I also made it accessible. You can use the whole app with just a keyboard."

**[Show the options screen again, or a simple sketch.]**
"But this is more than a screen. Imagine it connected to the railway's own systems. The moment a train's cancelled, we send you a link on SMS or WhatsApp — so you don't even have to go looking."

**[Show the "synthetic data" note on screen.]**
"Everything you saw today is test data, and this is an independent project — not made by Indian Railways."

"Thanks for watching."

## How to say it, not just what to say

- **Talk, don't recite.** Say it like you're explaining it to a friend over coffee, not presenting to a boss.
- **Use the pauses.** The dashes (—) and "..." are natural breathing points. Slow down there, don't rush through them.
- **Smile a little when you say "Hey, I'm Sid."** It carries into your voice even if no one sees your face.
- **Pause half a second after clicking something**, before you speak the next line. It looks calmer and keeps you in sync with the screen.
- **Don't aim for a perfect take.** If you stumble on a line, just re-record that one section.
- **Say numbers the way you'd say them out loud** — "over a megabyte," "under 300 kilobytes" — not like you're reading digits off a spec sheet.
