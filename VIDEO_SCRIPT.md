# Video script — talking to camera, no screen recording

You're recording this on your phone, facing the camera, with no app on screen.
So minute one becomes a **narrated walkthrough** — you describe exactly what
happens, step by step, as if you're walking a friend through it out loud. That's
the closest thing to a demo you can do without showing the screen.

> **One thing worth knowing:** the brief asks you to "demo the project as a
> citizen" and says judges want to see it work, not just hear about it. If your
> phone can screen-record (Control Center on iPhone, Quick Settings on Android),
> even a simple recording of you tapping through the app while you talk scores
> better than this version, for very little extra effort. If that's not
> workable right now, this script is written to make the spoken version as
> concrete and specific as possible instead.

Total spoken words: ~254. Even reading slowly and deliberately, that lands
between 1:40 and 1:55 — comfortably under the 2:00 limit.

---

### MINUTE 1 — walk through the app, out loud

"Hey, I'm Sid, and I built something called RailSathi."

"Here's the problem: your train gets cancelled, and all you get is a text message. Then you're on your own, figuring out what to do next."

"With RailSathi, I just open it and type in my PNR. No login, no password."

"One tap, and it tells me clearly: my train's cancelled, and my money is safe."

"Then it gives me three real choices — take another train, get a full refund, or ask someone to call me."

"I choose another train. It shows the time, the seat, and any extra cost — before I confirm."

"One tap, and I'm done. New ticket booked, refund already on its way."

"And the part I'm proud of — the whole app also works in Hindi, not just a few translated words."

### MINUTE 2 — how it was built

"Now, quickly — how I built this. I used Codex for almost everything: the code, the screens, all of it."

"Speed mattered a lot, because many people here are on slower connections. This started at over a megabyte. Now it's under 300 kilobytes."

"It's also accessible — you can use the whole thing with just a keyboard."

"But this is more than a screen. Imagine it connected to the railway's own systems — the second a train's cancelled, we send you a link over SMS or WhatsApp. You don't have to go looking."

"One honest note: everything I described is test data, and this is an independent project — not affiliated with Indian Railways."

"Thanks for watching."

## Delivery tips for a talking-head video

- **Look at the lens, not the screen.** Eye contact with the camera is what makes it feel like you're talking to the viewer, not reading notes.
- **Use your hands.** When you say "I open it" or "I tap," it's fine to gesture like you're holding and tapping a phone — it helps the viewer picture what you're describing, even though they can't see it.
- **Slow down on the specific details** — the PNR, the three choices, the numbers. Those are the moments that make it sound like a real product and not a pitch.
- **Don't aim for a perfect take.** If you stumble, just re-record that one section — you don't need to nail it in one go.
- **Say numbers the way you'd say them out loud** — "over a megabyte," "under 300 kilobytes" — not like you're reading digits off a spec sheet.
